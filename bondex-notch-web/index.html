Everything at the top of the screen

Five widgets, one surface. Each one answers a question you'd otherwise have to open an app to answer.

Music

Track, artist, album art and a scrubbing progress bar for Music and Spotify — with transport controls that don't steal focus from what you're typing.

File activity

Watches your Downloads folder and reports transfers as they land, with live throughput. Click through to reveal anything in Finder.

Pro

System

CPU load, memory pressure, battery level and time remaining, and real network throughput — sampled straight from the kernel, not estimated.

Activity feed

A running log of what changed: tracks, finished downloads, power events, shelf drops. Transient events also flash as a banner beside the notch.

Drop shelf

Drag files onto the notch to park them, then drag them back out anywhere. Nothing is copied — the shelf holds references to your real files.

Pro

Genuinely light

Swift and SwiftUI throughout, no Electron and no web view. Widgets you switch off stop sampling entirely, so they cost nothing.

Three states, one gesture

  1. 1

    Collapsed

    Exactly the size of your hardware notch, and completely invisible against it. Bondex is doing nothing to your screen.

  2. 2

    Peek

    When something is live — a track playing, a download finishing — a thin strip grows either side of the notch. Never over it.

  3. 3

    Expanded

    Move the pointer to the notch and the full panel springs down. Move away and it's gone. Click to pin it open while you work.

No notch? No problem.

On external displays and pre-2021 Macs, Bondex draws its own pill in the same place and behaves identically. It follows the built-in display when you plug in, and re-measures itself whenever your display arrangement changes.

On the roadmap

Straight answers

Which music apps are supported?

Apple Music and Spotify. macOS has no public system-wide "now playing" API — MPNowPlayingInfoCenter only reports the calling process, and the private framework that used to work was locked down in macOS 15.4. Bondex uses each app's scripting interface instead, which is the supported route. You'll be asked for Automation permission the first time.

Does it show my other apps' notifications?

No, and no Mac app can. Notification Center's database is protected by System Integrity Protection and there's no API to read it. Rather than pretend otherwise, Bondex shows an activity feed: what it observes directly — track changes, completed downloads, power events, shelf drops.

How much memory does it actually use?

It's a small Swift agent with no web view and no background helpers. Sampling is throttled and widgets you disable stop sampling entirely. Check it yourself in Activity Monitor — that's the honest answer.

What permissions does it ask for?

Three, all optional and all requested only when the relevant widget is on. Automation for Music and Spotify, Files and Folders to watch Downloads, and Notifications if you want Bondex to post its own alerts. Nothing leaves your Mac.

Will it work on my display?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel. Notch or no notch — on displays without one, Bondex draws a matching pill in the same position.
